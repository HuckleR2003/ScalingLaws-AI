# Scaling Laws

An AI company tycoon in Unity. The campaign starts on 1 January 2022 with twelve million dollars
and no product, and runs through the model race that followed.

Status: simulation and first screens are working, 127 EditMode tests pass, two scenes build. There
is no art yet and no player build yet.

---

## The idea

Upgrades are not bought, they are timed.

Hardware ages. An accelerator bought on launch day loses value to calendar time and again to every
successor that ships after it, so capital committed too early sits in an asset that is worth less
every month. Token prices fall by roughly half a year, permanently. The frontier moves whether or
not you do.

There is no guaranteed profit. A company that ships one model and coasts is insolvent inside three
years, and a test asserts that it is.

## What is simulated

**Model quality** comes from the Chinchilla parametric loss form, using the corrected fit rather
than the constants printed in the original paper, because those do not reproduce the paper's own
compute-optimal ratio. Around twenty tokens per parameter is optimal, and ten times the compute buys
exactly 10.0 capability points at every scale. Undertrain or overtrain and the same bill buys a
worse model.

**Hardware** is 22 real generations of accelerator, host CPU, node memory and fabric, with ship
dates, launch prices, board power and on-package memory. Buying an H100 at launch in October 2022
and holding it to June 2026 returns about 14 percent of the money. Buying a B200 in January 2025 and
selling on the same day returns about 53 percent.

**Compute** is rented by the petaflop or owned outright. Renting always tracks whatever the clouds
offer, 180 days behind launch, and never ages. Owning is roughly a third of the price per FLOP and
bills whether the cluster is busy or idle.

**Rivals** are eight agents, seeded from the real 2022 to 2026 release timeline and then free to
deviate. A patient lab that sees better silicon landing shortly after its planned launch will hold,
train on the newer hardware and come out measurably stronger. Ship into that window and you lead for
a season before being overtaken by something that waited on purpose.

**Research** is a tree of 17 nodes across four eras. Every architecture, corpus, upgrade line and
compute tier sits behind one. Money is a bad gate because money compounds; a node also costs
calendar, and calendar cannot be compounded away.

**Capital** is priced on proximity to the frontier to the fourth power, plus revenue at a multiple,
multiplied by investor sentiment. Sentiment runs from 0.55 in early 2022 to 2.20 in mid 2025. The
same company is worth four times as much depending only on when the term sheet was signed.

## Running it

Unity 6000.4.11f1.

```
-batchmode -nographics -projectPath <project> -runTests -testPlatform EditMode -testResults TestResults.xml
-batchmode -nographics -projectPath <project> -executeMethod ScalingLaws.Editor.ScalingLawsSceneBuilder.BuildAll -quit
```

Do not add `-quit` to `-runTests`. Unity finishes the import, never runs the tests, and exits 0.

Both scenes are generated by the editor builder and are in build settings. Nothing in them is hand
authored, so anything edited in the scene view is lost on the next rebuild.

## Layout

| Folder | Role |
|---|---|
| `Assets/_ScalingLaws/Scripts/Core/` | Date, clock, random, units. No game rules, no UnityEngine. |
| `Assets/_ScalingLaws/Scripts/Data/` | Data libraries plus lookups. No economics, no state. |
| `Assets/_ScalingLaws/Scripts/Simulation/` | The rules. Also no UnityEngine, so tests run in under two seconds. |
| `Assets/_ScalingLaws/Scripts/Persistence/` | Save format, migrations, PlayerPrefs. The only folder importing UnityEngine outside UI. |
| `Assets/_ScalingLaws/Scripts/UI/` | UI Toolkit panels built in C# against one stylesheet. |
| `Assets/_ScalingLaws/Editor/` | Scene generation. |
| `Assets/_ScalingLaws/Tests/EditMode/` | 127 tests. None of them load a scene. |

`Docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` is the mechanism map. Read it before adding anything, and extend an existing
catalog rather than starting a second one.

`Docs/UI_RESEARCH_AND_ASSETS.md` is the brief for whoever makes the art, including a list of the 26
icons needed and where to get them.

## Testing

```
Assets/_ScalingLaws/Tests/EditMode/
```

Eleven fixtures. Two of them do work the others cannot:

`PlayabilityTests` runs a scripted baseline player, deliberately ordinary, for four campaign years.
It asserts the player survives, ships models, stays within reach of the frontier, beats a company
that shipped once and coasted, and does not end up dominating. It found three balance faults that no
unit test would have: rental billed by unit count tripled on a hardware generation change, trait
decay made a shipped model worthless in two years for no decision the player made, and serving at
full training-active parameters made market share decorative because everyone was capacity bound.

`ConsistencyTests` checks that nothing anywhere produces a NaN, a negative where a negative is
nonsense, a value outside its own declared range, or a dangling reference between two catalogs. It
found a research node that opened six months before its own prerequisite, which made it permanently
unreachable.

## Notes

This project is openly AI assisted. Prose in the repository is written to be read as engineering
notes: no em-dashes, no marketing adjectives, real numbers only.

Company and product names in the game are deliberate near misses of real ones. Hardware
specifications and competitor release dates are public vendor and product information, rounded, and
are used as reference data for a simulation.
